# Substack Deep-Dive Writer

Use this skill to write high-signal, human-centric technical essays, newsletter posts, and engineering breakdowns designed for Substack subscribers and email-first readers.

## When to Use

- The user wants to write an authentic, commentary-driven Substack post or newsletter issue.
- The user asks to transform technical concepts into engaging, human-written deep dives with strong narrative voice, email hooks, and community CTA elements.

## Substack Human-Centric Style & Formatting Rules

1. **Email Header & Metadata:**
   - Always provide a compelling **Subject Line** (under 60 characters) and a **Preview Text / Subtitle** (under 120 characters).
   - State estimated reading time (e.g., `8 min read | 2,100 words`).

2. **Authentic Human Voice & Narrative:**
   - **First-Person & Direct Address:** Write as a practitioner sharing real battle scars from production ("When we deployed this...", "I spent 3 hours debugging...").
   - **No AI Buzzword Clichés:** Strictly eliminate corporate filler words like *"delve," "game-changer," "testament," "unlocking," "realm,"* and *"paradigm shift."*
   - **Show Trade-offs & Failures:** Highlight where the solution fails, cost implications, and edge cases. Authentic engineering honesty drives reader retention.

3. **Substack-Specific Layout Elements:**
   - **ASCII / Plain-Text Diagrams:** Use clear ASCII boxes/arrows or standard Markdown quotes for architecture workflows so they render reliably in all email clients.
   - **Section Dividers:** Use clean `---` dividers to pace the read.
   - **Inline CTAs:** Integrate organic reader interaction buttons like `[ Leave a comment ]`, `[ Share this post ]`, and `[ Subscribe ]`.
   - **ASCII / Clean Formatting Tables:** Avoid complex tables that break on mobile email screens; use clean ASCII borders or key-value bullet lists.

## Execution Workflow

1. **Narrative & Thesis Definition:**
   - Establish the "Why Now" hook, the core technical lesson, and the human perspective/experience behind the story.

2. **Structural Composition:**
   - **Subject Line & Subtitle**
   - **The Personal Hook / Production Context:** Open with an relatable engineering reality or late-night scenario.
   - **The Core Thesis:** Contrast the status quo with the proposed approach.
   - **The Technical Deep Dive:** Code snippets, architectural trade-offs, and implementation details.
   - **The Verdict & Lessons Learned:** What to keep in mind before adopting this pattern.
   - **Newsletter Engagement CTAs:** Natural closing invitation for comments and subscriber discussion.
